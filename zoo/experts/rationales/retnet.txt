**Problem Addressed:** Transformers face a fundamental trade-off between training parallelism and inference efficiency. While attention enables parallel training through its parallel representation, it requires O(N) complexity per step during inference due to the need to attend to all previous tokens. This makes deployment of large language models computationally expensive and memory-intensive, particularly for long sequences. The challenge is to design an architecture that maintains training parallelism while achieving O(1) inference complexity without sacrificing model performance.

**Key Innovation - Multi-Scale Retention Mechanism:** RetNet introduces the retention mechanism as a replacement for softmax attention, which can be formulated in three equivalent ways: parallel, recurrent, and chunkwise recurrent. The core token mixer replaces softmax normalization with exponential decay along the sequence dimension, enabling a recurrent formulation. Specifically, retention computes: `o_t = Σ exp(q_t k_i^T) v_i / Σ exp(q_t k_i^T)` in parallel form, but can be reformulated as `S_t = S_{t-1} + k_t^T v_t; o_t = q_t S_t` in recurrent form. The multi-scale aspect uses different decay rates (gamma values) across heads for capturing patterns at different temporal scales, with each head applying GroupNorm before concatenation rather than LayerNorm on the full output.

**Implementation Details:**
- The retention mechanism supports three computation paradigms that are mathematically equivalent but computationally distinct
- Parallel representation enables efficient training with full sequence parallelization
- Recurrent representation achieves O(1) inference by maintaining a fixed-size state matrix
- Chunkwise recurrent combines both: processes chunks in parallel while maintaining recurrence between chunks
- Multi-head retention uses head-specific decay parameters for multi-scale temporal modeling
- Removes softmax's numerical instability issues while maintaining expressiveness

**SNN Compatibility & Expected Impact:** RetNet's recurrent formulation with explicit state transitions makes it naturally compatible with SNN conversion. The retention mechanism's exponential decay can be mapped to leaky integrate-and-fire neuron dynamics. By replacing dense linear projections (q_proj, k_proj, v_proj, o_proj) with SpikingLinear layers, the model gains event-driven sparsity while preserving its O(1) inference advantage. The fixed-size state matrix (d_k × d_v per head) enables efficient neuromorphic implementation. The hybrid approach maintains the parallel training benefits while the spiking conversion primarily targets the inference pathway, making RetNet an ideal candidate for energy-efficient deployment on neuromorphic hardware with its combination of linear complexity and sparse computation.
