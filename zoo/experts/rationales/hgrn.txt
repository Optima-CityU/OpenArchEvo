**Problem Addressed:** Traditional RNNs with gating mechanisms (like LSTMs and GRUs) suffer from slow training due to sequential dependencies, while Transformers achieve parallel training but have quadratic complexity. Linear RNNs offer O(N) complexity and parallel training through associative scans, but early variants lacked data-dependent gating mechanisms, limiting their ability to selectively retain or forget information. The challenge is to design a linear RNN with data-dependent gates that can be efficiently trained in parallel while maintaining strong sequence modeling performance.

**Key Innovation - Hierarchical Gating with Data-Dependent Forget Gates:** HGRN introduces a minimal gated linear recurrence with data-dependent forget gates organized hierarchically. The core token mixer implements: `S_t = α_t ⊙ S_{t-1} + (1-α_t) ⊙ (k_t v_t^T)` where `α_t = σ(x_t W_α)` is a data-dependent forget gate computed from the input. The hierarchical structure processes information at multiple temporal scales: lower layers capture short-term dependencies with faster forgetting (smaller α), while higher layers maintain long-term context with slower forgetting (larger α). This creates a hierarchy of timescales without explicit architectural changes—the data-dependent gates naturally learn appropriate forgetting rates for each layer.

**Implementation Details:**
- Data-dependent forget gate: `α_t = σ(x_t W_α)` adapts forgetting based on input content
- Scalar forget gate per channel: `α_t ∈ R^d` provides per-dimension control
- State update: `S_t = α_t ⊙ S_{t-1} + (1-α_t) ⊙ (k_t v_t^T)` where ⊙ is element-wise multiplication
- Parallel training via associative scan algorithm: enables sequence-level parallelization
- Vector-valued states: S_t ∈ R^d (one scalar per channel, not matrix-valued)
- Minimal parameterization: only adds forget gate parameters W_α
- Hierarchical behavior emerges from learning: different layers learn different timescales

**SNN Compatibility & Expected Impact:** HGRN's data-dependent gating with hierarchical timescales maps naturally to spiking neural dynamics. The forget gate `α_t = σ(x_t W_α)` can be implemented using spiking neurons with adaptive thresholds, where input spikes modulate the effective leak rate of integrate-and-fire neurons. Converting all linear projections (W_α, k_proj, v_proj, output projections) to SpikingLinear layers introduces event-driven sparsity. The element-wise gating `α_t ⊙ S_{t-1}` can be realized through gain modulation in spiking networks, where one population of neurons modulates the activity of another. The hierarchical timescale structure naturally aligns with biological neural systems where different neuron types have different membrane time constants and refractory periods. The vector-valued state (one scalar per channel) is memory-efficient for neuromorphic implementation. The resulting Spiking-HGRN combines the adaptive temporal dynamics of data-dependent gating with the energy efficiency of sparse, event-driven computation. The hierarchical organization, when implemented through heterogeneous populations of spiking neurons with diverse temporal properties, enables multi-timescale processing similar to biological neural systems. This makes Spiking-HGRN particularly suitable for neuromorphic implementations of sequence models where both short-term and long-term dependencies must be captured efficiently, with the data-dependent gating enabling adaptive information retention based on input-driven spike patterns.
