Idea:
The evolution from Version 2 focuses on two key areas to enhance the spiking neural network nature and improve architectural clarity and consistency, particularly within the DeltaNet attention mechanism.

### 1. Streamlined and Consistent Activation Handling in DeltaNet
**Problem in Version 2:** The handling of activation functions for `q`, `k`, and `v` in `DeltaNet` was potentially redundant and inconsistent. Specifically, `F.silu` could be applied unconditionally if `use_short_conv` was `False`, followed by another `qk_activation` block, leading to double activations or unexpected behavior.

**Implemented Solution:**
- Removed the `else` branch for `self.use_short_conv` that applied `F.silu(q), F.silu(k), F.silu(v)` unconditionally. The primary non-linearity for `q, k, v` is now derived from the `SpikingLinear` layers themselves. `ShortConvolution` layers also apply their own internal activations if configured.
- Introduced a dedicated `self.v_activation = nn.SiLU()` for the `v` projection when `use_short_conv` is `False`. This ensures that `v` always receives a non-linear activation similar to how `v_conv1d` behaves (which has `activation='silu'`).
- Centralized the application of `self.qk_activation` for `q` and `k` *after* any potential `ShortConvolution` layers but *before* the rearrangement and `l2norm`/`sum_norm`. This ensures that `qk_activation` is applied exactly once and in a controlled manner, making the flow of non-linearity explicit and consistent.

**Motivation:** This modification significantly improves the clarity and correctness of the activation application within `DeltaNet`. By centralizing and de-duplicating activations, we ensure a more predictable signal flow, which is crucial for the stable training and interpretability of hybrid SNN-ANN models. It removes potential sources of over-activation and allows the `qk_activation` parameter to precisely control the desired non-linearity in `q` and `k` vectors.

### 2. Spike-Based Beta Modulation in DeltaNet
**Problem in Version 2:** While `beta` was generated by a `SpikingLinear` layer, its subsequent use in `delta_rule_chunkwise` did not directly integrate other spike activity information. The existing `beta` primarily acted as a learned analog scaling factor, missing an opportunity to embed more direct neuromorphic dynamics into the core attention mechanism.

**Implemented Solution:**
- Introduced a new `SpikingLinear` layer, `self.spike_beta_mod_proj`, within `DeltaNet`'s `__init__` method. This layer takes a single input dimension and projects it to `self.num_heads`.
- In the `forward` pass, the mean spike activity of the `k_proj` output (`spikes_k_proj.float().mean(dim=-1, keepdim=True)`) is calculated. This scalar value (per token) represents the overall local spike intensity from the keys.
- This mean spike activity is then fed into `self.spike_beta_mod_proj`, which (as a `SpikingLinear` layer) processes it through its internal neuron and linear transformation to produce a `beta_spike_mod` tensor of shape `(B, L, num_heads)`. This `beta_spike_mod` is then collected as part of `all_spikes`.
- The original `beta` (from `self.b_proj`) is then modulated multiplicatively by `(1 + beta_spike_mod.tanh())`. Using `tanh` scales the modulation factor around 1, allowing `beta` to be slightly increased or decreased based on spike activity without large swings.
