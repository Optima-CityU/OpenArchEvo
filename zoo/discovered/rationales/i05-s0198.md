Idea:
The primary motivation for this architectural evolution stems from the observed performance improvements in [Version 2] over [Version 1], particularly regarding the increased reliance on spiking neurons for non-linearity. [Version 2] demonstrated gains by removing explicit ANN activation functions (like GELU in SpikingMLP and specific activations in DeltaNet's forward pass) and the `spike_beta_mod_proj` mechanism. This indicates that a more direct and 'pure' spiking neural network approach for non-linearity is beneficial.

Building upon this insight, the key architectural modification in this version is the removal of the `qk_activation` parameter and its associated assertions from the `DeltaNet`'s `__init__` method. In [Version 2], while the `qk_activation` parameter was present in `__init__`, its corresponding logic to apply explicit non-linear activations (e.g., F.silu, relu, elu_p1) in the `forward` method after `q_proj`, `k_proj`, and `v_proj` had already been removed. Furthermore, the `ShortConvolution` layers were modified to have `activation=None`. This meant `qk_activation` had become a vestigial parameter, asserted but functionally ignored.

Removing this unused parameter simplifies the `DeltaNet`'s interface, clarifies the architecture's intent, and reinforces the design principle that non-linearity in the projection layers (`q_proj`, `k_proj`, `v_proj`) and, by extension, the entire `DeltaNet`, is now solely derived from the spiking neuron models within the `SpikingLinear` layers. This leads to a cleaner, more efficient spiking neural network design by eliminating unnecessary parameter overhead and potential confusion, aligning more closely with the objective of emphasizing efficient spiking neural network nature and discouraging superfluous ANN operations. The `SpikingMLP` remains purely spike-driven, with non-linearity entirely encapsulated within its `SpikingLinear` components, as refined in [Version 2]. The spike collection and regularization mechanism introduced in [Version 2] is retained as it contributes to training stability and sparse activity, which are desirable SNN characteristics.
