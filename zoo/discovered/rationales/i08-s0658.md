Idea:
The current `SpikingFLABlock` (Version 2) has already made significant strides by adopting the `SS4D` (Spiking State Space Model) as its core. This moves from attention-like mechanisms to a state-based approach, inherently improving long-range dependency handling and maintaining linear complexity, which aligns with the "locality and statefulness of computation" objective. The consistent use of `SpikingLinear` ensures that all linear layer inputs are binary signals, fulfilling the core SNN requirement.

My improvement builds upon this solid foundation by refining the normalization strategy within the block. Specifically, I've replaced `nn.LayerNorm` with `RMSNorm`.

**Why RMSNorm?**
1.  **Efficiency**: RMSNorm calculates root mean square, which is a simpler operation than LayerNorm (which computes both mean and variance). This can lead to minor computational speedups, especially critical in resource-constrained neuromorphic contexts.
2.  **Stability**: In some modern transformer architectures (e.g., Llama), RMSNorm has been found to offer similar or even improved training stability compared to LayerNorm.
3.  **SNN Alignment**: Spiking Neural Networks are often more sensitive to the exact distribution of activations. RMSNorm, by only normalizing the scale (not subtracting the mean), might preserve more of the underlying signal structure that could be beneficial for spiking neuron dynamics. It's a "lighter" normalization that fits the general philosophy of keeping SNN operations efficient and less complex than traditional ANNs.

This change is focused and compliant:
*   It does not alter the `SpikingLinear` class.
*   It maintains the `SpikingFLABlock` class name and its `__init__` and `forward` signatures.
*   It ensures all inputs to `nn.Linear` layers within `SpikingLinear` are binary (as per `SpikingLinear`'s internal mechanism).
*   It continues to return `final_spikes` in the required concatenated, flattened tensor format.
*   It preserves chunked processing (handled by SS4D's design) and causal integrity.
*   It maintains sub-quadratic complexity.
*   It replaces an existing component (`LayerNorm`) with an improved alternative (`RMSNorm`) rather than adding bloat.
*   It ensures batch-size agnostic operations via `einops` and dynamic shape handling.

This evolution aims to marginally improve efficiency and potentially stability, reinforcing the core SNN principles within a high-performing sequence modeling architecture.
