Idea:
The evolution from Version 1 to Version 2 showed that simplifying the architecture by removing dropout between spiking linear layers in the `SpikingMLP` yielded performance improvements. Building on this evidence, this iteration extends that simplification by removing dropout layers (`dropout1`, `dropout2`) from the main `SpikingFLABlock`. In Spiking Neural Networks, the inherent sparsity of binary spike signals can serve as a form of regularization, potentially making explicit dropout redundant or even detrimental in certain locations, especially when signals are already binarized.

Simultaneously, to maintain robust control over the spiking dynamics and ensure an 'efficient spiking neural network nature' as per the objective, a configurable `spike_reg_weight` has been reintroduced into `SpikingFLABlock`. This allows for explicit regularization of the overall spike activity across the entire block, providing a crucial knob to manage firing rates and prevent runaway activity, which is a common challenge in SNNs. This centralized regularization, combined with the spike-based beta modulation already present in `DeltaNet` (which was a key improvement in Version 2), creates a more balanced approach to SNN stability and performance.

The removal of dropout layers simplifies the forward pass, potentially reducing computational overhead and leading to more direct and stable propagation of spike-encoded information. The re-introduction of global spike regularization ensures that while the architecture is simplified, its core SNN characteristics are controlled and optimized. This design aims to leverage the inherent properties of SNNs (sparsity) while providing explicit mechanisms for controlling their dynamics (firing rate regularization and adaptive beta modulation) for improved cognitive capabilities and efficiency.
