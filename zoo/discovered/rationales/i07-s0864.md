Idea:
The previous `SpikingMLP` (Version 2) contained a critical inconsistency regarding the input type expected by `SpikingLinear`. The `SpikingLinear` class is designed to receive an *analog* input, which it then processes through its internal spiking neuron to generate binary spikes for its `nn.Linear` sub-layer. In Version 2, the `self.fc2` layer within `SpikingMLP` was directly receiving *binary spikes* (`x_after_dropout_spikes`). This meant `self.fc2`'s internal spiking neuron was attempting to generate spikes from an already binary input, leading to redundant or potentially ill-defined behavior.

To address this, the improved `SpikingMLP` ensures that all `SpikingLinear` layers receive analog inputs, adhering strictly to the defined contract of the `SpikingLinear` class. The key modification is as follows:

1.  **Analog Output Gating**: After the first `SpikingLinear` layer (`self.fc1`) produces its analog output (`x_fc1_out_analog`), this analog signal is then processed by an `intermediate_spike_activation` neuron to generate *binary spikes* (`intermediate_spikes`).
2.  **Dropout on Spikes**: Dropout is applied to these `intermediate_spikes` to induce further sparsity, resulting in `sparse_intermediate_spikes` (still binary).
3.  **Spike-Controlled Analog Gating**: Instead of directly feeding these `sparse_intermediate_spikes` to `self.fc2`, we use them to *gate* the *original analog output* of `self.fc1`. This is achieved by an element-wise multiplication: `gated_analog_input_for_fc2 = x_fc1_out_analog * sparse_intermediate_spikes`. This operation transforms the sparse binary spikes into a sparse *analog* signal, which is then fed into `self.fc2`.

This design change ensures:
-   **Strict Adherence to `SpikingLinear` Contract**: Every `SpikingLinear` instance (in both `LightNet` and `SpikingMLP`) now consistently receives an analog input for its initial spike generation phase, resolving the architectural inconsistency.
-   **Preservation of Spiking Nature**: The explicit `intermediate_spike_activation` neuron, a key enhancement from Version 2, is retained. Its role evolves from a direct feeder to a controller, introducing spike-driven sparsity directly into the analog signal path.
-   **Enhanced Efficiency and Sparsity**: By gating the analog signal with binary spikes, the model leverages the inherent sparsity of SNNs to potentially reduce computational load and improve representational efficiency, aligning with neuromorphic computing principles of sparse activity.
-   **Maintainability and Clarity**: The corrected flow makes the SNN architecture more theoretically sound and easier to reason about, as the behavior of `SpikingLinear` is consistently applied.

Additionally, the redundant `use_spiking_mlp` parameter in `SpikingFLABlock.__init__` has been removed, streamlining the API as the MLP is now inherently spiking.
