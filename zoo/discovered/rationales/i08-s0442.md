Idea:
Version 2 of the SpikingFLABlock architecture already introduced significant improvements over Version 1 by integrating spike-gated residual connections and dynamic beta modulation based on spike activity, pushing towards a more truly neuromorphic design. These innovations allowed the network to adapt its information flow and contextual memory based on the actual firing patterns of its spiking neurons, leading to superior performance. 

Building upon this success, the primary motivation for this enhanced version is to further refine the mechanism of spike-dependent residual gating within the Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) block. In Version 2, the MLP residual was modulated by a gating factor derived from the *mean* spike activity across both MLP sub-layers (fc1 and fc2). While effective, this approach treats the MLP block as a single entity regarding its spiking signature for gating.

Our architectural evolution aims to provide a more granular and informative control signal for the MLP residual connection. Instead of a single averaged spike activity, the new design introduces a `mlp_combined_spike_gate_proj` layer that takes as input the *concatenated mean spike activities* of both `fc1` and `fc2` sub-layers independently. This allows the gating mechanism to differentiate between the firing rates of the first and second MLP stages.

**Key Architectural Innovation and Implementation:**
1.  **Enhanced MLP Spike Gating Layer (`mlp_combined_spike_gate_proj`):**
    *   **Old (Version 2):** A `SpikingLinear(nn.Linear(1, d_model))` layer was used, taking a single scalar (mean of combined MLP spike activities) and projecting it to `d_model` dimensions for gating.
    *   **New (Current Implementation):** A `SpikingLinear(nn.Linear(2, d_model))` layer is introduced. Its input is formed by concatenating the mean spike activity of `mlp_spike1` (from `fc1`) and `mlp_spike2` (from `fc2`) along the feature dimension, resulting in a `(B, L, 2)` tensor. This provides distinct signals from each MLP sub-layer.
2.  **Differentiated Gating Signal:** By providing separate mean spike activities for `fc1` and `fc2` to the new `mlp_combined_spike_gate_proj`, the network can learn a more sophisticated and layer-specific modulation for the MLP residual connection. This allows the gating factor to respond to the unique neuronal engagement profiles of each MLP sub-component.

**Theoretical Grounding and Expected Improvements:**
This modification is theoretically grounded in the principle of dynamic, activity-dependent control, a hallmark of neuromorphic computing. Biological neural networks exhibit complex feedback mechanisms where the activity of one part of the network can dynamically modulate the processing in other parts. By allowing the MLP residual gate to distinguish between the firing patterns of `fc1` and `fc2`, we enable:
*   **Richer Contextual Adaptation:** The network can dynamically adjust the impact of the MLP's output on the overall block based on whether, for instance, the first expansion layer (`fc1`) or the second projection layer (`fc2`) is more active. This could be crucial for adapting to different input complexities or information processing stages.
*   **Improved Information Flow Control:** More fine-grained gating provides better control over the flow of processed information through the residual path, potentially reducing redundancy or emphasizing salient features based on the `d_ffn` layer's spiking behavior.
*   **Enhanced Representational Capacity:** By allowing the residual pathway to be selectively weighted by the internal dynamics of the MLP, the model gains a more adaptive and flexible representational capacity, which is expected to translate into improved modeling capabilities for sequence tasks.

All modifications maintain sub-quadratic complexity, adhere to batch-size independence, uniformly use `SpikingLinear` for linear transformations preceded by spike neurons, and ensure `final_spikes` is a flattened tensor of binary spike activity. The `DeltaNet`'s input unpadding logic was also slightly refined for greater robustness and adherence to dynamic dimension handling.
