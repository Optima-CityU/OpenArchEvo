Idea:
The evolution from Version 1 to Version 2 indicated that a simplified approach to spike-driven modulation, where `beta` and `gate` parameters are directly derived from the `hidden_states` via `SpikingLinear` layers, rather than being additionally modulated by aggregated spike activity, led to improved performance. This suggests that over-complicating spike-based dynamic control might introduce unnecessary complexity or instability, especially with the current `SpikingLinear` design which already ensures binary signals into the linear projections. 

This new version, `SpikingFLABlock_regulated`, builds upon the successful architectural simplifications observed in Version 2, particularly in the `DeltaNet` component. It removes the `use_spike_beta_modulation` and `use_spike_gate_modulation` parameters and their associated spike-driven projection layers, relying instead on the inherent spiking mechanism of the `SpikingLinear` projections for `beta` and `gate` generation. This maintains a cleaner, more direct computational flow while still adhering to the core SNN principle of binary inputs to linear layers.

Furthermore, a critical SNN training component, spike activity regularization, has been re-introduced and integrated. While Version 2 demonstrated performance gains, it implicitly removed this regularization. Regularization of spike activity (e.g., aiming for sparse or balanced firing rates) is crucial for stable and efficient SNN training, preventing runaway activity or dead neurons. By re-introducing `spike_regularization_weight` and computing a `total_reg_loss` based on the mean firing rate of all collected spikes (`final_spikes`), this architecture aims to combine the performance benefits of Version 2's simplified modulation with enhanced SNN training stability and control. This ensures that the network's cognitive capabilities are not only driven by effective connectivity but also by biologically plausible and controlled spiking dynamics.

Finally, a redundant parameter `use_spiking_mlp` in `SpikingFLABlock`'s `__init__` method was removed for better code clarity, as `SpikingMLP` is consistently used.
