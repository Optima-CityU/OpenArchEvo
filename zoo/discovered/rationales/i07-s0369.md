Idea:
The "Spiking Co-Activation Unit" (SCU) enhances the SCM-FFN by replacing a simple element-wise spike multiplication with a learned, spike-driven combination. This allows the network to develop more complex, non-linear interactions between the 'main' and 'modulation' spike pathways, moving beyond a basic logical AND operation. The SCU concatenates the two spike trains and processes them through a dedicated SpikingLinear layer followed by a SpikingPostNormActivation. This enriches the feature integration within the FFN, enabling a broader range of learned co-activations and dynamic feature representations.

The "FFN Local Residual Gating" (FLRG) introduces a more granular and context-specific control over the FFN's residual connection. Previously, the block-level Adaptive Unified Spiking Gate (AUSG) provided global residual gates for both the SS4D and FFN sub-blocks. With FLRG, the FFN now generates its own local, spike-driven forget and update gates based on its immediate input. This design allows the FFN to adaptively control its information flow and integration with its residual path, independent of the SS4D's gating decisions. The AUSG's residual gates are now dedicated exclusively to the SS4D path, promoting a cleaner separation of concerns and more focused adaptation for each major sub-block. This adherence to 'high cohesion and low coupling' principle enhances modularity and enables finer-grained control over information dynamics within the SpikingFLABlock, potentially leading to more efficient and robust learning.
