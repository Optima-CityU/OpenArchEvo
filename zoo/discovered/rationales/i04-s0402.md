Idea:
The evolutionary design focuses on two key aspects to enhance the SpikingFLABlock:

1.  **Enhanced Expressive Power in SpikingMLP via GELU Activation:**
    *   **Problem Identification:** In Version 2, the `SpikingMLP` simplified its structure to `SpikingLinear -> Dropout -> SpikingLinear`. While each `SpikingLinear` module internally incorporates a non-linear spiking neuron, a direct activation function between the two primary linear transformations in an MLP block is a common and critical element for enhancing representational capacity in traditional ANNs. The absence of such an explicit activation in the analog domain, particularly after the output of the first `SpikingLinear` and before the second, could limit the MLP's ability to learn complex feature transformations.
    *   **Implementation:** An `nn.GELU()` activation function has been reintroduced within the `SpikingMLP` class. Specifically, after the analog output of `self.fc1` (which itself processes spikes internally), the `GELU` activation is applied, followed by dropout, before feeding into `self.fc2`. The modified flow is `SpikingLinear (internal spikes) -> GELU (analog) -> Dropout (analog) -> SpikingLinear (internal spikes)`.
    *   **Motivation:** This modification significantly boosts the `SpikingMLP`'s non-linear processing capabilities, allowing it to capture more intricate patterns. By placing `GELU` on the analog output of the first spiking layer, we maintain the integrity of the spiking operations at the core of `SpikingLinear` while leveraging a potent ANN non-linearity to enrich the feature space before the subsequent spiking-enabled linear transformation. This hybrid approach aims for superior cognitive capabilities without sacrificing the neuromorphic computing nature.

2.  **Streamlined Activation Path in DeltaNet for SNN Consistency:**
    *   **Problem Identification:** Version 2 of `DeltaNet` had conditional `qk_activation` and `v_activation` blocks that applied additional ANN-style non-linearities (`F.silu`, `relu`, `elu_p1`) to the analog `q`, `k`, and `v` tensors *after* they had already passed through `SpikingLinear` modules (and potentially `ShortConvolution` layers which might also have internal activations). This created redundancy and a less clean architectural philosophy where the primary non-linearity was expected to derive from the spiking neurons themselves.
    *   **Implementation:** The explicit `else:` block within `DeltaNet.forward` that applied `qk_activation` and `v_activation` (e.g., `F.silu(q)`, `F.silu(k)`, `self.v_activation(v)`) when `self.use_short_conv` was `False` has been completely removed. Correspondingly, `self.v_activation` in `DeltaNet.__init__` has also been removed, as its purpose was tied to this redundant activation path.
    *   **Motivation:** This change simplifies the `DeltaNet`'s activation landscape. The non-linearity for the `q`, `k`, and `v` projections now primarily and consistently originates from the internal spiking neurons within their respective `SpikingLinear` layers. If `ShortConvolution` is enabled, its internal activations (if configured) still apply. By removing these redundant post-`SpikingLinear` ANN activations, the architecture better reflects a clean hybrid SNN-ANN design where spiking neurons are the main source of non-linearity for linear transformations, leading to a more efficient spiking neural network nature and clearer information flow. This fosters a design featuring high cohesion and low coupling by clearly delineating where spiking non-linearities are introduced.
